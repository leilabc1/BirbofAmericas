Cardinal
Cardinalis cardinalis

  • Located mostly Kansas, Nebraska, Miss ouri, Minnesota, in Iowa, North Dakota and South Dakota
  • Males can be identified by their entirely red body, a crest on the top of their head and long tail feathers. Females can be identified by their warm toned brown feathers with red streaks on their wings and tail feathers
  • Diet: Primarily granivorous, eating seeds, grains, wild fruits, and berries, especially favoring sunflower seeds at backyard feeders. They also eat insects, which are critical for feeding their growing nestlings.

Bluejay
Cyanocitta cristata

  • Located in: Blue jays live across eastern and central North America, ranging from southern Canada down to Florida and northeastern Texas, primarily east of the Rocky Mountains
  • Bluejays are identifiable by their pointed head crests and striking sky blue colorations. They also have dark stripes on their wings and tail feathers as well as a white face and belly
  • Diet: In the warmer months, roughly two-thirds of their diet consists of insects, including caterpillars, beetles, ants, and wasps. In fall and winter, they shift toward seeds, nuts, and acorns, frequently visiting backyard bird feeders for sunflower seeds, peanuts, and suet.

Robin
Erithacus rubecula

  • Located in: Spans across North America, from Alaska and Canada down to Mexico.
  • American robins have dark gray/black head with a bright yellow bill and white partial eye-rings as well as an orange/red belly. The rest of their bodies are grey. Females are typically duller.
  • Diet: Forages on the ground for earthworms, caterpillars, and grasshoppers, mostly in the morning but can also eat seeds and plant matter

Red Winged Blackbird
Agelaius phoeniceus

  • Located in: Found across most of North America, stretching from Alaska and Canada down to Costa Rica
  • Male red winged blackbirds have glossy jet-black feathers with striking red-and-yellow shoulder patches called epaulets that they flash during territorial displays. While females are dull, streaky brown and buff plumage with a pale eyebrow, making them look somewhat like large sparrows so they stay hidden while nesting.
  • Diet: About three-fourths of their annual diet consists of seeds and waste grain such as weed seeds, corn, wheat, while the rest includes insects like beetles, caterpillars, grasshoppers, and snails, especially during the summer

Great Horned Owl
Bubo virginianus

  • Located in: Found across North America, Central America, and South America, stretching from the Arctic down to Tierra del Fuego.
  • Features prominent feather tufts on its head resembling horns or ears, large yellow eyes, a white throat patch, and mottled grayish-brown or reddish-brown barred plumage
  • Diet: A generalist predator, feeding primarily on mammals and birds such as rabbits, hares, mice, and voles, but they are also one of the few regular predators of skunks. They consume smaller prey whole and regurgitate indigestible bone and fur pellets.
