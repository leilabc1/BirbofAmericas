Northeast Region

Tufted Titmouse
Baeolophus bicolor


  • Located in: Found year-round across the eastern and midwestern United States, extending north into parts of New England and southern Canada
  • Tufted Titmouse is easily recognized by its prominent gray crest with a black beak area, large black eyes, and clear "peter-peter-peter" whistle.
  • Diet: In the warmer months, roughly two-thirds of their diet consists of insects, including caterpillars, beetles, ants, and wasps. In fall and winter, they shift toward seeds, nuts, and acorns, frequently visiting backyard bird feeders for sunflower seeds, peanuts, and suet.

American Goldfinch
Spinus tristis


  • Located in: Found across North America, ranging from Canada down through much of the United States and into Mexico. They are partial migrants, moving short distances south for the winter to areas where January temperatures stay above 0°F.
  • Spring and summer males feature a brilliant yellow body, black forehead, and black-and-white wings. Females and winter birds are a duller grayish-brown or olive color.
  • Diet: Strictly vegetarian (granivores), eating almost exclusively seeds from sunflowers, nyjer thistle, and various weeds

Mourning Dove
Zenaida macroura
  • Located in: Found across open country, farmlands, urban areas, suburbs, and forest clearings, frequently seen perched on telephone wires or foraging on the ground.
  • Mourning doves are Slaty-brownish to buffy-tan above with distinctive black spots on the wing coverts and behind the eyes.
  • Diet: They are granivores and eat exclusively seeds and plant matter

White Throated Sparrow
Zonotrichia albicollis
  • Located in: Spends the summer breeding season in northern coniferous and mixed forests across Canada and parts of the northern United States. Migrates south for the winter, inhabiting brushy edges, woodlots, thickets, and suburban parks across the eastern and southern United States, as well as California
  • The white throated sparrow features a stark white throat patch, a yellow spot (lore) between the eye and the bill, and a dark bill.
  • Diet: Eats primarily insects and spiders, including beetles, caterpillars, and snails. Shifts to a diet of weed and grass seeds, and berries during the winter.

Scarlet Tanager
Piranga olivacea
  • Located in: Mature deciduous and mixed forests with a high preference for oaks across eastern North America. And in winter move to lowland tropical rainforests and forests along the Andes in northern and central South America.
  • Male scarlet tanagers have brilliant red bodies with contrasting jet-black wings and tails. Females have dulled yellowish-green backs and yellow bellies with dark-brown or brownish wings.
  • Diet: Their diet consists of mostly insects, seeds, berries, and plant matter
