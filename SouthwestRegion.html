Greater Roadrunner
Geococcyx californianus
  • Located in: Primarily found in arid, open, and semi-arid regions of the southwestern and western United States as well as Mexico.
  • The greater roadrunner is identified by their spikey black head feathers, long black tail feathers, and long slender legs.
  • Diet: The greater roadrunner is a voracious, opportunistic omnivore whose diet consists of insects and arthropods, reptiles, amphibians, small mammals, and birds.

Gambel's Quail
Callipepla gambelii

  • Located in: Found mainly in Arizona, southeastern California, southern and central New Mexico, far western Texas, southern Utah, and small parts of Nevada and Colorado.
  • The gambel’s quail can be identified by their black head crest. They have short round grey/brown bodies to blend into their surroundings. The males often have a red brown head with a white a white stripe lining their black throats, while females markings mostly blend into their bodies
  • Diet: The Gambel’s quail primary eats berries, leaves, buds and seeds as they are most vegetarian.

Curve-billed Thrasher
Toxostoma curvirostre
  • Located in: Mainly found in Arizona, New Mexico, Colorado and Kansas and mainly found in arid scrublands, brushlands, and cactus-laden plains.
  • Diet: Curve bill thrashers are known for their long sharp beaks that they use to forage and eat insects, amber golden eyes as they mature into adulthood and grey speckled plumage that cover their entire grey/cream bodies.

Gilded Flicker
Colaptes auratus
  • Located in: Found across nearly all of North America, parts of Central America, and Cuba. Northern populations migrate short distances south for the winter.
  • Brownish bodies with bold black scalloping on the back and black spots on the belly, plus a prominent black crescent on the chest. Red on the faces of the males.
  • Diet: Primarily eats ants and beetles, consuming thousands of ants daily. They use a slightly curved bill to probe into anthills and the ground, and a long sticky tongue that can dart out two inches. They also eat fruits and seeds in the winter
