Purple Finch
Haemorhous purpureus

  • Located in: The purple finch is entirely native to North America, distributed across two distinct regional subspecies with seasonal migration patterns. They are common across the North, East, and Pacific seaboard, but are notably rare or absent throughout most of the Rocky Mountains
  • They are primarily identified by their raspberry-red or old-rose wash covering the head, breast, back, and rump, often famously described as looking like a sparrow dipped in raspberry juice.
  • Diet: while seeds are their primary food source, they also eat berries, buds, and insects.

Common Grackle
Quiscalus quiscula

  • Located in: tehy are mostly found extensively across North America east of the Rocky Mountains.
  • The common grackle is a large, slender North American blackbird known for its glossy, iridescent plumage, bright yellow eyes, and long, keel-shaped tail.
  • Diet: Opportunistic omnivores. They eat agricultural grains like rice and corn, seeds, insects, small frogs, mice, bird eggs, and even garbage. They are known to dunk dry food in water to soften it!

Red-bellied Woodpecker
Melanerpes carolinus

  • Located in: Found widely across the central and eastern United States, ranging from Florida up to the Great Lakes and parts of southern Canada.
  • Features a striking black-and-white zebra-striped or barred pattern on its back and wings. They also have a distinct red head and red/white face and belly.
  • Diet: these birds feed on insects, spiders, arthropods, acorns, nuts, seeds, and fruits. They readily visit backyard bird feeders for suet, peanuts, and sunflower seeds.
  • *soundbite*

Carolina Chickadee
Poecile carolinensis

  • Location: Found year-round across the southeastern and central United States, stretching from New Jersey westward to Texas.
  • They are very small birds known for their grey bodies and black and white heads and throats.
  • Diet: these birds feed on insects, spiders, seeds, and berries, and regularly visits backyard bird feeders for sunflower seeds, suet, and peanuts.
